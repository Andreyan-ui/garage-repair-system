Safety first

What should I do if my car breaks down on the road?
  1. Switch on your hazard lights and move as far off the road as you safely can.
  2. On a busy or fast road, get everyone out on the side away from traffic and stand well back from the car.
  3. Place your warning triangle (life saver) about 30 metres behind the car. Never stand in the road to do it.
  4. Note a landmark near you, such as a fuel station, shop or school, so we can find you quickly.
  5. Call us or send a breakdown request with your location.
Call us instead if:

Always. This is exactly what our breakdown service is for. If anyone is hurt, call 999 or 112 first.

Starting and battery

My car won't start and I hear a clicking sound

A fast clicking sound usually means the battery is too weak to turn the engine.

  1. Switch off the lights, radio and air conditioning, then try again.
  2. Open the bonnet and look at the battery terminals. If they are loose or covered in white or green crust, tighten them with the engine off and wipe them with a dry cloth.
  3. Try a jump-start from another car (see the jump-start guide).
  4. If the car starts, drive for at least 30 minutes without switching off so the battery can recharge.
Call us instead if:

It still only clicks after a jump-start, you smell burning, or the battery case is swollen or leaking.

How do I jump-start my car safely?

You need jumper cables and a second car with a good battery.

  1. Park the cars close together without touching. Switch both engines off and apply the handbrakes.
  2. Clip one red clamp to the + terminal of the flat battery, then the other red clamp to the + terminal of the good battery.
  3. Clip one black clamp to the - terminal of the good battery, then clip the last black clamp to bare, unpainted metal on your engine, away from the battery.
  4. Start the working car and let it run for 3 to 5 minutes, then try to start yours.
  5. Once your car is running, remove the clamps in reverse order: black from your car, black from the good car, red from the good car, red from your car.
Call us instead if:

The cables spark a lot or get hot, or your car still will not start after two or three tries.

The engine turns over but will not start

If the engine cranks normally but never fires, the cause is usually fuel or the key.

  1. Check the fuel gauge. Gauges can be wrong when the tank is nearly empty, so add a few litres if you are unsure.
  2. Make sure the car is in Park (automatic) or that the clutch is pressed all the way down (manual).
  3. Try your spare key. A flat key battery can stop the immobiliser from recognising the key.
  4. Do not crank for more than 10 seconds at a time. Wait a minute between tries so you do not flatten the battery.
Call us instead if:

You smell fuel, the car has been cranking for a long time, or it still will not start after these checks.

Tyres and wheels

I have a flat tyre. How do I change it?
  1. Pull over on flat, firm ground well away from traffic. Switch on your hazard lights and apply the handbrake.
  2. Place your warning triangle (life saver) about 30 metres behind the car.
  3. Loosen each wheel nut half a turn while the wheel is still on the ground.
  4. Put the jack under the jacking point shown in your owner's manual and raise the car until the tyre is just off the ground.
  5. Remove the nuts and the wheel, fit the spare, and tighten the nuts by hand.
  6. Lower the car, then tighten the nuts fully in a star pattern.
  7. Drive gently to a garage. Many spare wheels are not made for long distances or high speed.
Call us instead if:

You are on a busy highway or a bend, the ground is soft or sloping, the nuts will not loosen, or your spare is also flat.

How often should I check my tyre pressure?

Check your tyres once a month and before long journeys, when the tyres are cold.

  1. Find the correct pressure on the sticker inside the driver's door or in your owner's manual.
  2. Use the gauge at a fuel station to check each tyre, including the spare.
  3. Add air until each tyre matches the recommended pressure.

Under-inflated tyres wear out faster, use more fuel and make the car harder to control.

Call us instead if:

A tyre keeps losing air, has a bulge or cut on the side wall, or the tread is worn smooth.

Warning lights

What do the dashboard warning lights mean?

Red usually means stop soon. Amber or yellow means get it checked.

  • Red oil can: low oil pressure. Stop the engine as soon as it is safe.
  • Red thermometer: the engine is overheating. Stop and let it cool.
  • Red battery: the charging system has failed. The car will only run until the battery is flat.
  • Red brake symbol: make sure the handbrake is fully released. If the light stays on, the brake fluid may be low.
  • Amber engine outline: an engine fault has been recorded. You can usually drive gently to a garage.
  • Amber tyre with an exclamation mark: one of your tyres is low on air.
Call us instead if:

Any red light stays on, or the engine light is flashing. A flashing engine light means a misfire that can damage the car.

The check-engine light came on. Can I keep driving?

If the light is steady and the car drives normally:

  1. Make sure the fuel cap is tightened. A loose cap can turn the light on.
  2. Drive gently and avoid carrying heavy loads.
  3. Book a diagnostics scan so we can read the fault code.

If the light is flashing, or the car is shaking or losing power, stop driving.

Call us instead if:

The light is flashing, the car is shaking or losing power, or you can smell burning.

Engine and fluids

My engine is overheating. What should I do?
  1. Switch off the air conditioning and turn the heater to full hot. This pulls heat away from the engine.
  2. Pull over when it is safe and switch off the engine.
  3. Do not open the radiator cap while the engine is hot. The coolant is under pressure and can cause serious burns.
  4. Wait at least 30 minutes, then check the coolant level in the plastic overflow bottle.
  5. If the level is low, top it up with coolant, or clean water if that is all you have, to get you to a garage.
Call us instead if:

Steam keeps coming from under the bonnet, coolant is leaking under the car, or the temperature rises again soon after topping up.

How do I check my engine oil?
  1. Park on level ground and wait 5 minutes after switching the engine off.
  2. Pull out the dipstick (usually a yellow or orange handle), wipe it clean, push it all the way back in, then pull it out again.
  3. The oil should sit between the two marks. If it is near or below the lower mark, top up a little at a time with the oil grade listed in your manual.
  4. Oil that looks milky or smells burnt is a sign of a problem.
Call us instead if:

The oil looks milky, the level drops quickly between checks, or the oil warning light comes on.

How do I top up windscreen washer fluid?
  1. Open the bonnet and find the washer reservoir. Its cap usually shows a windscreen symbol.
  2. Fill it with clean water mixed with a little screen wash. Avoid washing-up liquid, which can damage paint and rubber seals.
  3. Close the cap firmly.
Call us instead if:

The pump makes a noise but no water sprays, or the reservoir empties without being used. It may be cracked.

Lights and electrical

My lights, radio or power windows suddenly stopped working

This is often caused by a blown fuse.

  1. Find the fuse box. It is usually under the dashboard or under the bonnet, and the lid shows which fuse does what.
  2. With the engine and lights off, pull out the fuse for the faulty item using the small plastic puller.
  3. A blown fuse has a broken wire inside. Replace it with a fuse of exactly the same amp rating (the number printed on top).
  4. Never fit a higher-rated fuse or wrap a fuse in foil. This can start a fire.
Call us instead if:

The new fuse blows again straight away. That means there is a short circuit that needs to be repaired.

My remote key has stopped working

A flat battery in the key is the most common cause.

  1. Use the metal key hidden inside the remote to open the door.
  2. Gently open the remote with a small screwdriver or coin and note the battery type printed on it.
  3. Fit a new battery with the + side facing the same way as the old one.
  4. If the car still will not start, hold the remote against the start button or ignition. Many cars can read the key even when its battery is flat.
Call us instead if:

A new battery does not help, or the car shows an immobiliser or key warning.
